1. APPLICATION PURPOSE



Build a professional crypto-market intelligence platform that combines:



1. Live order-book liquidity visualisation.

2. Historical market-data replay.

3. Spot versus perpetual futures comparison.

4. Liquidity migration research.

5. Market-integrity monitoring.

6. Empirical research and export tools.

7. AI-assisted research analysis.

8. Financial-regulation and market-transparency research.



The application must be useful as a genuine software-development portfolio and an independent research tool for postgraduate legal research.



Prioritise reliable functionality, data integrity, reproducibility and professional design.



2. DESIGN AND NAVIGATION



Create a professional, responsive financial-research interface.



Use a modern market-terminal aesthetic, with dark backgrounds where appropriate, clear chart labels, accessible controls and responsive layouts.



Create the following navigation sections:



- Overview Dashboard

- Live Heatmap

- Spot vs Perpetual

- Liquidity Migration

- Market Integrity

- Research Studio

- Regulatory Research

- AI Research Assistant

- Methodology & About



Display the application name prominently: Fz Heat Map Intelligence.



Use FZ HMI as the compact brand identifier.



The interface must work on desktop and Android mobile browsers.



3. OVERVIEW DASHBOARD



Create an informative overview dashboard containing:



- Selected trading instrument.

- Exchange and data-connection status.

- Current market price, when available.

- Bid and ask depth.

- Liquidity concentration indicators.

- Latest recorded market events.

- Research-session status.

- Data freshness and source information.



Every displayed statistic must come from a real data source or be explicitly labelled as simulated demonstration data.



Never invent live prices, trading volumes, order-book quantities or research results.



4. LIVE ORDER-BOOK HEATMAP



Implement a BTC/USDT heatmap using an authentic exchange market-data feed.



Features:



- Bid and ask liquidity visualisation.

- Price-versus-time heatmap.

- Current market-price marker.

- Liquidity concentration levels.

- Order-book depth inspection.

- Zoom, pan and timeframe controls.

- Timestamped market observations.

- Connection and data-quality indicators.

- Historical recording for future analysis.



Use a suitable exchange WebSocket API and reconstruct the order book from snapshots and incremental updates.



Handle disconnections, sequence gaps, stale messages and invalid updates.



If a live feed cannot be connected in the initial implementation, provide a clearly labelled demonstration mode and keep the data connector modular for later integration.



5. HISTORICAL HEATMAP AND REPLAY



Build a historical research viewer using recorded order-book data.



Allow users to:



- Select a recorded date and time.

- Replay market activity.

- Pause, resume and change replay speed.

- Inspect liquidity at individual price levels.

- Annotate market events.

- Compare conditions before and after an event.

- Export the observations used in an analysis.



Do not present fabricated replay data as historical market activity.



If historical data has not yet been collected, display a clear empty state and instructions for recording data.



6. SPOT AND PERPETUAL COMPARISON



Create synchronised spot and perpetual-market views.



Where reliable data is available, show:



- Spot order-book depth.

- Perpetual order-book depth.

- Bid/ask imbalance.

- Executed trades.

- Cumulative volume delta.

- Funding rates.

- Open interest.

- Liquidation data with source attribution.

- Time-aligned market events.



Allow users to compare the timing and magnitude of changes between markets.



Clearly distinguish observed correlations from causal explanations. Spot/perpetual divergence alone must not be labelled manipulation.



7. LIQUIDITY MIGRATION RESEARCH ENGINE



Make this the principal research feature of the new application.



Build tools to investigate how displayed liquidity changes across price levels and time.



Track:



- Initial displayed liquidity.

- Order additions and cancellations.

- Liquidity persistence.

- Changes in depth.

- Price distance from selected liquidity zones.

- Reappearance of liquidity at different levels.

- Market behaviour around selected events.

- Subsequent price acceptance or rejection.



Provide configurable definitions for candidate liquidity sweeps, post-sweep acceptance and rejection.



Allow researchers to adjust thresholds, time windows and observation periods.



Do not assume that liquidity disappearing at one level and appearing at another represents the same order or participant.



Support research into Liquidity Destination Theory and the Hierarchy of Liquidity Magnets as hypotheses requiring empirical testing.



Each research event must contain:



- Event ID.

- Instrument and exchange.

- Timestamp.

- Data source.

- Event-definition parameters.

- Recorded observations.

- Relevant charts.

- Researcher annotations.

- Data-quality warnings.

- Alternative explanations.

- Limitations.



Support reproducible analysis and CSV/JSON exports.



8. MARKET INTEGRITY MONITOR



Create a monitoring module that identifies unusual order-book patterns.



Investigate:



- Rapid additions and cancellations.

- Large displayed orders with short persistence.

- Repeated liquidity appearances and disappearances.

- Changes in depth around price movements.

- Relationships between displayed liquidity and executed trades.

- Repeated patterns around predefined price levels.



Use cautious descriptions such as “unusual order cancellation pattern” or “potential spoofing indicator.”



Do not identify a pattern as proven fraud or manipulation without sufficient independent evidence.



Display the data supporting each alert, the detection thresholds and relevant limitations.



9. RESEARCH STUDIO



Build a workspace where researchers can:



- Define research questions.

- Select instruments and observation periods.

- Configure event-detection rules.

- Save and annotate research sessions.

- Compare recorded events.

- Re-run analyses using saved parameters.

- Export data and reports.

- Record methodology and software version.



Calculate appropriate descriptive statistics only when supported by sufficient data.



Where valid, report sample sizes, distributions, missing-data rates, sensitivity to thresholds and false-positive estimates.



Never fabricate statistical significance, predictive accuracy or research findings.



10. REGULATORY RESEARCH WORKSPACE



Create a dedicated section titled Market Transparency & Regulatory Research.



Support investigation of:



- Institutional versus retail access to market information.

- Technological asymmetry.

- Cryptocurrency market transparency.

- Market-surveillance challenges.

- Evidence retention and auditability.

- Limitations of order-book evidence.

- Investor protection.

- Potential regulatory responses.



Provide a structured research template covering:



1. Research question.

2. Relevant observations.

3. Data and methodology.

4. Alternative explanations.

5. Evidentiary limitations.

6. Applicable legal framework.

7. Potential regulatory implications.

8. Further evidence required.



Verify legal sources before presenting them as authoritative. Distinguish current law, regulatory guidance, academic literature and proposed reforms.



11. AI RESEARCH ASSISTANT



Integrate an AI research assistant that can interpret actual recorded data.



It should help users:



- Summarise a selected event.

- Explain metrics and analytical methods.

- Compare recorded events.

- Identify missing data and alternative explanations.

- Draft preliminary research notes.

- Suggest testable hypotheses.

- Explain potential regulatory relevance.



Ground outputs in available observations. Clearly disclose uncertainty and cite relevant internal data records or verified external sources.



Do not fabricate market data, legal authorities or citations.



If an AI API is required, build a secure integration point and explain how to configure it. Never expose private API keys in client-side code.

Implement:



- Data-source attribution.

- Accurate timestamps.

- Connection-status indicators.

- Stale-data warnings.

- Missing-data detection.

- Error handling and reconnection.
